This development continuation implements slice C's initial retained-node and

## Public journey

`item.related()` returns a link view with readable labels and declared `role`,
`target_ref` and `availability` cells. It includes retained source statements,
relationship subjects/objects, original assertion subjects and exact glossary
entry references. Arbitrary scalar values, names, sequences and extension mappings
are not identity instructions. Structural entries reuse their original relationship
and assertion support. A subfield of a relationship keeps that record's endpoints.

The link view's origin is the item's detail view, whose origin is the exact
selected/ordered source view. `link.open()` follows explicitly;
`links.follow(item_ref)` is an exact-result alias. Filters use declared columns;
missing/ambiguous destinations remain distinct from source coverage. Link
explanations retain original support and the derived rule
`retained_context_links@1`; they never become SourceAssertions.

A supplied destination opens its own captured profile. Its explorer's subsequent
views preserve the transition origin. `back()` restores the link view and then the
original detail/selection; cycles record actual steps without recapturing inputs
or recursively expanding Cards. Original caller Cards can change without affecting
this context. Related exploration retains the original supplied input list and
capture registry, with an independently explicit focal capture. Source assertion
links open the exact original record, not a provider URL.

## Identity and revisions

Only exact Card/entity references, exact pinned record references, and retained
source-supported `same_as` edges admit destinations. Every identity edge retains
its original relationship location, present assertion locations and missing support.
Derived-only, possible-identity, sequence/name similarity and orthology do not
establish joins. Unsupported/conflicting identity edges remain recorded as blocked
paths. Other stored identity representations remain inspectable; they do not gain
an implicit navigation rule.

The connected supported identity graph is retained rather than expanding every
cyclic path. All agreeing and conflicting edges survive; no source-backed route is
discarded just because a direct match also exists. Several candidate Cards or
revisions yield `ambiguous`, with one labeled link per distinct captured pin.
Opening a candidate explicitly selects that destination for navigation, without
scientifically reconciling its values. Input order never chooses a revision.
Identical repeated inputs keep one capture and every supplied input occurrence.
A pin never falls back to another snapshot or an unpinned/latest state. A referenced
record absent from a supplied pinned Card is `missing_prerequisite`.

A mentioned destination without a supplied Card yields `context_not_supplied`;
a path without retained source basis yields `missing_prerequisite`. The view keeps
the known target, available glossary entry and original link/support. Its public
`explorer`, focal Card pin, snapshot and profile are null. Actual contributing
captures remain explicit; no empty destination Card or foreign fields are invented.
Typed inspection of the retained context remains available, and `back()` returns
to the originating links. These are operational outcomes, never biological absence.

## Shared access and cost boundary

Switching focus reuses the one-time captures and literal result/location registry.
Each focal access has independent selection/cursor bindings; structural caches
include the focal capture, preventing another Card's result from leaking across
navigation. Identity graph and target resolution are cached within this captured
context. Opening, representation and links make no scientific provider requests.
Scientific topic materialization remains an explicit local reader operation under
its existing rule/cache contract. No new schema, dependency or identity resolver is
introduced. The generic topic catalog now describes its literal quality route
without promising scientific coverage classification.
